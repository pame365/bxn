PLAN AHEAD

When do you file?

Deadlines can vary by tax year, filing status, business type, and circumstances. Confirm the exact due date each year.

Late January

The IRS filing season generally opens around this time. Gather forms as they arrive.

April 15

Usual federal individual income tax deadline, subject to calendar-year changes and special circumstances. Verify New York deadlines each year.

March–April

Many business returns are due during this period; the exact deadline depends on entity type and tax year.

All year

Estimated payments, payroll, sales tax, notices, and prior-year filings can have deadlines outside tax season.

Need more time?

A filing extension may give you more time to file, but generally does not extend the time to pay taxes owed. Ask about eligibility and timing before the deadline.

A LITTLE PREPARATION GOES A LONG WAY

Practical tax tips.

General information to help you stay organized. Your filing obligations depend on your facts and current rules.

Keep your records together

Save income statements, receipts, charitable records, childcare or education documents, and business expense records in one secure place.

Report all income

Wages, freelance work, gig income, interest, and other sources may have reporting requirements — even if you did not receive a particular form.

Check credits and deductions

Credits such as the Earned Income Tax Credit or Child Tax Credit may be available if you meet current eligibility rules.

Separate business finances

Track business income and ordinary, necessary expenses carefully. Keep receipts and mileage records; do not claim personal expenses as business costs.

Review before you file

Check names, Social Security numbers, bank details, dependents, and every page before signing or authorizing e-filing.

Do not ignore a tax notice

Read the notice, note the response date, and get help promptly. Keep a copy and use the response method on the official notice.

A clear view of the numbers starts with organized records.

Plan ahead and ask questions before deadlines.

KNOW WHAT IS AT STAKE

Why filing matters.

Filing a complete and accurate return can help you meet obligations and claim benefits you may qualify for.

Possible refund or credits

A return may be needed to claim a refund or eligible credits.

Financial records

Filed returns can help document income for certain applications; requirements vary.

Late-filing consequences

If required to file and you miss the deadline, the IRS and/or New York may assess penalties and interest. Amounts depend on your situation and applicable rules.

Late payment

Interest and penalties may apply to unpaid tax, even if you have an extension to file.

Filing requirements differ, and a refund may have a time limit for claiming. Get advice based on your circumstances.
